Canoeing Cedar Creek

by

Bob Whaley

1982


I have dreamed of canoeing Cedar Creek for a number of years. On a winter day, about 5 years ago, Jon Deboer and I scouted the upper section on foot from route 609 and when it began to snow, we hiked out at Natural Bridge. We were thrilled over the prospect of someday canoeing this stream. Later, I hiked most of the lower section alone, until some shooting in the woods made me abandon my scouting trip.

One day some years later, thinking there would be enough water, Will Summers, Chip Queitzsch, and I went to the put-in. Cedar Creek was not quite high enough that day (even though the Maury was running at six and one half feet in the pass), so we settled for Kerrs Creek and Mill Creek.

The during the last week of May, 1982, we had a lot of rain, and I began to think again of Cedar Creek, which is a little over an hour’s drive from my home in Albemarle County. The rain came down hard; my driveway washed into the street; my basement flooded; my hopes rose. As June arrived, the rain continued. Friday afternoon, June 4th, more heavy rain! Tomorrow was the right day to run Cedar Creek, and Olie Fordham agreed to join me in yet another adventure.

Luckily my son, Robert, and his friend, Susan, were, visiting in my home that same weekend, and they were willing to run shuttle, be helpful, and take pictures in that section of Cedar Creek accessible by the paths at Natural Bridge. On the advice of my attorney they were to purchase four tickets to see Natural Bridge – two for them and two for Ollie and me, so we could not be charged with trespassing as we paddled through the Natural Bridge property.

Saturday morning, we arrived at the put-in (Route 609) and decided the water was just right. (It proved, however, to be lower than ideal, scrapey, but runable.)

Cedar Creek is beautiful. It tilts down hill from the beginning, past an old mill and over a number of steep, straight forward ledges for about two miles.

After paddling those two miles, we came to Lace Falls, which is about fifty feet high. It is sloping, and when I had seen it previously, without water, it looked as if it might develop into something runable. Looking at it with water is another story. We did not even consider running Lace Falls. Our portage was river right, up the hill, along the hill and slide the canoes down.

At the bottom of Lace Falls, we saw the first natural Bridge tourists on the paths beside the river. They seemed to enjoy the spectacle of canoes in this setting, and they were busily taking pictures. Robert and Susan were also there taking pictures of us, as they had promised.

With tourists looking on, we paddled the fast water, interrupted by two short portages over bridges. Then we were under Natural Bridge. I thought of Thomas Jefferson’s words as he described this bridge (which he once owned), “… so beautiful an arch, so elevated, so light, springing as it were up to heaven!”

After passing under Natural Bridge, we quickly paddled to the low dam beside the concession stand, portaged this dam and then paddled around the bend. I was relieved that no had taken exception to our passage. I had been afraid to ask permission of the owners of Natural Bridge for fear the answer would be, “No.” Now, that worry was over, and, instead of being on our way to jail, Ollie and I were continuing our journey down Cedar Creek.

We paddled the back water of a high dam – probably thirty feet on top of a natural ledge another twenty feet high. Portage was an easy slide down on the right bank. The remainder of the trip had excellent white water, two tree portages, and a bridge portage which was about 300 yards from the James River. We took out at the junction of the James.

This is a beautiful trip. I would estimate the drop to be over 100 feet per mile. The scenery is excellent – uncluttered wilderness before and after Natural Bridge. For dessert, Ollie and I ran Goshen pass on the way home.
